## 4. Research clusters

Record counts below are approximate keyword classifications over the 53 deduplicated records.

### C1 — Output-only system identification, operational modal analysis (OMA) and SHM of dams (≈19 records, 2014–2026) — **strongest; active**

- **Phenomena:** modal behaviour of large water-retaining structures under ambient, forced and seismic excitation; damping estimation; damage-induced changes in modal properties.
- **Structures:** arch dams (Shahid Rajaee, Karun III, Karun IV, Pacoima), a gravity dam (Koyna, in S01) and generic multi-degree-of-freedom (MDOF) benchmarks.
- **Techniques:**
  - Frequency-domain methods: FDD + continuous wavelet transform (T01, T03).
  - Subspace methods: balanced SSI (T05, T09); automated SSI-COV with fuzzy clustering (T10).
  - Transmissibility methods: weighted transmissibility (T07, T08, S02); multi-reference weighted Hermitian transmissibility (T11); PSD-transmissibility complex frequency (S01).
  - Supporting techniques: time-delay correction for unsynchronised sensors (T03); XFEM + wavelet damage detection (T06).
- **Experimental:** the first full-scale forced-vibration test of an arch dam in Iran (T02) and ambient tests at Karun IV (T04).
- **Status:** the most cited and most continuous line. The newest papers (T10, T11, S01; 2025–2026) are all here, with new co-authors (Amanzad, Hafezi).

### C2 — Seismic wave scattering, topographic site effects and spatially varying (non-uniform) input for dams (≈16 records, 2007–2023) — **strong; tapering**

- **Phenomena:** canyon amplification; time delay and coherency of ground motion at topographic sites; non-uniform support excitation of arch dams.
- **Methods:**
  - 3D frequency- and time-domain BEM (in-house codes TDASC and BEMSA);
  - symbolic regression for time-delay and coherence functions (T13–T15);
  - random-vibration analysis;
  - validation against Pacoima Dam records.
- **Status:** the PhD-era foundation (T12 and the 2007–2009 BEM papers), developed at length with M. Isari's group in 2019–2023. No 2024–2026 records were found.

### C3 — Underground structures and seismic wave scattering (≈2–4 records, 2019–2023) — peripheral

Subway stations under near- and far-fault motions, studied with uniform and non-uniform excitation (S03; NMCE 2023).

### C4 — Hydraulic structures and water engineering (≈9 records, 2019–2026) — **emerging; active**

- **Spillway cavitation:** 2-phase VOF with standard k-ε at Aydoghmush dam (T16).
- **Gates and weirs:** discharge-coefficient (Cd) prediction from laboratory data with SVM/ANN/KNN (T17, labyrinth sliding gates with sills) and ANFIS/M5P/GPR (S06, combined weirs).
- **Gabion weirs:** experiments plus CFD with a turbulence-model comparison (T18).
- **Bottom outlets:** mesh zoning and grid-convergence (GCI) methodology for CFD (T19).
- **Embankments:** seepage metaheuristics (Applied Sciences 2020) and first-filling settlement of the Sabalan earth dam (2021).
- **Character:** focused on **time-averaged hydraulic performance** (Cd, discharge, cavitation index), using laboratory data, soft computing and steady or 2D CFD.

### C5 — Fluid–structure interaction and extreme loading of dams and tanks (≈5 records, 2015–2024) — secondary

- Dam–reservoir interaction by the Legendre spectral element method (S04).
- Seismic response of base-isolated liquid tanks (S05).
- Blast loading (Modares Civil Eng. J. 2018) and underwater explosion (T20) at Karun 4.

### C6 — Miscellaneous (≈2–4 records) — peripheral, not used for topic selection

Machine-learning prediction of concrete strength, a geopolymer soil-stabilisation preprint, and similar.

| 2024–2026 | Next-generation transmissibility OMA (Amanzad) and automated SSI (Hafezi, Pourgholi); hydraulic structures: gates, weirs, outlets (Abbaszadeh; Vaseti/Akhtari) | T10, T11, T17–T19, S01 |

**Current centre of gravity (2024–2026):**

1. Output-only identification methods for seismic and ambient responses of dams.
2. Hydraulic control structures: gates, weirs and outlets.

These two active lines are **not yet connected** in the verified record. The hydraulic papers address discharge and flow performance; they do not address dynamic loading or structural response. The OMA papers address dam bodies under seismic and ambient input; they do not address flow-excited hydraulic components.

## 7. Recurring unresolved limitations (attributed)

| # | Limitation | Source records | Type |
|---|---|---|---|
| L1 | New identification methods are validated mainly on numerical models or a single structure/earthquake | T01, T03, T06, T07, S01 | Stated or evident from abstracts |
| L2 | OMA assumes white-noise, stationary inputs; the residual uncertainty comes from violating this | T10 | **Explicitly stated** |
| L3 | Short, non-stationary seismic records cause spectral leakage and dispersion near poles; closely spaced and higher modes are hard to separate | T07, T08, T11, S01 | **Explicitly stated** (motivation of the papers) |

## 8. Implication for topic selection (no gaps or topics proposed here)

The evidence points to three areas where doctoral topics would sit most naturally with this supervisor:

- **C1**, the strongest and most active line;
- **C4**, the newest and most active applied line;
- the **unexploited junction between C1 and C4**: the dynamics of water-interacting hydraulic components.

C2 is a strong historical line, but it is tapering. Candidate gaps are listed separately in `02_Preliminary_Research_Gaps.md`.
